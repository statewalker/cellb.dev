A browser runtime's OPFS pool (`sahpoolOpener`, used by `browserProjectRuntime`) grows ahead of its cells instead of once it is full. Each new slot is an OPFS file with a sync access handle, made one at a time, and in a busy worker each one takes seconds, about 5 s with 32 Flue conversations in one worker. Before, the pool counted only the files that already existed, so it did not see the journals open cells were about to create. Its background growth ran from a microtask that ran late under load. And a cell that found the pool full waited for it to double (64 more handles, minutes), which looked like a hang: 32 conversations in one worker stalled. Now an open cell counts its journal until it has one; every open (a failed one too) starts the background growth, which keeps two thirds of the pool free; a growth step asks for its 16 slots in parallel (about one slot's wait instead of sixteen); and a cell waiting for room waits for what was needed when it asked, not for later reservations.
